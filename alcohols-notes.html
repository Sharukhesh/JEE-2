B. Dehydration of Alcohols

conc. H₂SO₄ at 170°C (443 K) E1 Mechanism

Gives Saytzeff (Zaitsev) Alkene (more substituted, trans > cis). Rate ∝ Carbocation stability (3° > 2° > 1°). Ring expansion & 1,2-shifts ALWAYS occur if a more stable cation can form.

conc. H₂SO₄ at 140°C (413 K) SN2 (1° Only)

Gives Symmetrical Ether (R–O–R) with excess 1° alcohol. 2° and 3° alcohols still give alkenes via E1!

ThO₂ (Thorium Oxide), Δ Hofmann Alkene

Gives less substituted (Hofmann) alkene without carbocation rearrangement.

POCl₃ + Pyridine, 0°C E2 (Anti-Elimination)

Clean E2 dehydration — ZERO carbocation rearrangement.

C. Pinacol-Pinacolone & Victor Meyer Test

Pinacol–Pinacolone Rearrangement Vicinal Diol + conc. H₂SO₄, Δ

1. Protonate & remove the –OH that leaves the most stable carbocation (Ph₂C⁺ > Ph(Me)C⁺ > Me₂C⁺).

2. 1,2-Shift driven by back-donation of remaining oxygen's lone pair (C=O⁺H octet completion).

Migratory Aptitude: –H > –Aryl (EDG) > –Phenyl > –Aryl (EWG) > 3° Alkyl > 2° Alkyl > 1° Alkyl > –CH₃

Semi-Pinacol Trap: With C(OH)–C(NH₂) + HNO₂, –NH₂ ALWAYS leaves as N₂ regardless of carbocation stability!

Part 03 // Ethers & Oxiranes

Ethers (R–O–R') & Epoxides: Synthesis & HI Cleavage Rules

Williamson • HI Decision Tree • Epoxides

Williamson Ether Synthesis

Strict SN2
R–O⁻Na⁺ + R'–X → R–O–R' + NaX (R'–X MUST be 1° or CH₃–X)
✅ CORRECT (To make tert-butyl methyl ether):
(CH₃)₃C–O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃Br → (CH₃)₃C–O–CH₃ + NaBr (SN2)
❌ WRONG (3° Halide undergoes E2 Elimination!):
CH₃O⁻Na⁺ + (CH₃)₃C–Br → CH₂=C(CH₃)₂ (Isobutene) + CH₃OH + NaBr

Alternative for hindered ethers: Use Alkoxymercuration-Demercuration (Alkene + 1. Hg(OAc)₂, ROH / 2. NaBH₄) → Markovnikov ether without elimination or rearrangement!

Unsymmetrical Epoxide Ring Opening

Anti-Opening (Inversion)
1. Basic / Nucleophilic Medium (Pure SN2) RO⁻, OH⁻, RMgX, LiAlH₄, CN⁻

Nucleophile attacks the LESS sterically hindered carbon (1° > 2° > 3°) with Inversion.

2. Acidic Medium (Borderline SN2 / δ⁺ TS) H₂O/H⁺, ROH/H⁺, HX

Nucleophile attacks the MORE substituted carbon (3°, Benzylic, Allylic) that best stabilizes the δ⁺ charge in the transition state (still proceeds with Inversion!).

Cleavage of Ethers with HX (HI > HBr > HCl): The 4-Rule JEE Decision Tree

Step 1 is ALWAYS fast protonation: R–O–R' + HI → R–O⁺(H)–R' + I⁻

#1 Most Asked JEE Topic
RULE 1: Both R & R' are 1° or 2° Alkyl SN2 Pathway

Halide (I⁻) attacks the LESS sterically hindered group:
CH₃–O–CH₂CH₃ + HI (1 eq, cold) → CH₃I + CH₃CH₂OH
*(If Excess Hot conc. HI is used: converts both to iodides → CH₃I + CH₃CH₂I + H₂O).*

RULE 2: One Group is 3°, Benzylic, or Allylic SN1 Pathway

Cleaves to form the stable carbocation (3°, PhCH₂⁺), so halide attaches to the 3°/Benzylic group:
(CH₃)₃C–O–CH₃ + HI → (CH₃)₃C–I + CH₃OH
Ph–CH₂–O–CH₃ + HI → Ph–CH₂–I + CH₃OH

RULE 3: Alkyl Aryl Ethers (Anisole Ph–O–R) sp² C–O Never Breaks

The Ph–O bond has partial double-bond character. ALWAYS gives Phenol + R–I (even with excess hot HI, Phenol never forms Ph–I!):
Ph–O–CH₃ + HI (even excess) → Ph–OH + CH₃I
*(Note: Diphenyl ether Ph–O–Ph does NOT cleave with HI at all!)*

RULE 4: Anisole Electrophilic Substitution –OCH₃ = o/p-Director

• Anisole + Br₂ in CH₃COOH → p-Bromoanisole (90% Major) (stops at monobromination, unlike Phenol!).
• Undergoes smooth Friedel-Crafts Alkylation/Acylation to give 4-Methoxyacetophenone (Major).

Part 04 // Quick-Check Vault

Master Acidity Order & NaHCO₃ / FeCl₃ Distinction Rules

pKa & Effervescence Rules

Complete JEE Acidity Order (Ka)

Picric Acid (2,4,6-Trinitrophenol) > R–COOH > H₂CO₃ > p-Nitrophenol > o-Nitrophenol > m-Nitrophenol > Phenol > CH₃OH > H₂O > 1° R–OH > 2° R–OH > 3° R–OH > HC≡CH
1. NaHCO₃ Test Rule

Liberates CO₂↑ ONLY if stronger than H₂CO₃:
• R–COOH & Picric Acid / 2,4-DNP: Give brisk effervescence of CO₂ (carbon of CO₂ comes from NaHCO₃!).
• Phenol & Alcohols: NO reaction with NaHCO₃.

2. aq. NaOH Solubility

• Phenol: Dissolves in aq. NaOH to form PhONa (and is re-precipitated by bubbling CO₂ + H₂O!).
• Aliphatic Alcohols: Insoluble/do not react with NaOH (react only with active Na, K, NaH).

3. Neutral FeCl₃ & Liebermann Test

• Neutral FeCl₃: Phenol gives Violet/Purple complex [Fe(OPh)₆]³⁻.
• Liebermann Nitroso Test: NaNO₂ + conc. H₂SO₄ → H₂O → NaOH gives Blue/Green → Red → Deep Blue.
